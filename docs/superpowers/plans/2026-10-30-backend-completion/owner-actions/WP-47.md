# WP-47 owner actions: tax, billing address and portal configuration

The code is on branch `wp/47`. It builds the recommended path for decision 7, "USD, Stripe Tax on", behind `STRIPE_AUTOMATIC_TAX`, which defaults to off. Deploying it changes nothing for customers until you do step 7. The full rules are in `docs/runbook/billing.md`.

What changed for you:

- With `STRIPE_AUTOMATIC_TAX=1`, Checkout computes tax on top of the USD prices, requires a billing address and offers a tax id field (VAT, GST). With it at `0`, Checkout is exactly as before.
- The customer portal is now a script you run, `npm run stripe:portal -w @auto-career/server`. It shows what would change, and writes only with `--apply`. It encodes the WP-10 step 8 portal settings: the nine prices, downgrades at period end, and cancel at period end. It adds immediate invoicing of upgrades, and lets customers edit their address and tax id.

Order: steps 1 and 2 are decisions, steps 3 to 6 are test mode, and step 7 is live. Do not set `STRIPE_AUTOMATIC_TAX=1` anywhere before Stripe Tax is active in that mode. Otherwise every checkout fails with `503 billing_unavailable`.

## 1. Confirm decision 7 (decision)

Reply with one of:

- **"USD, Stripe Tax on"** (recommended). Do steps 2 to 8.
- **"Merchant of record"** (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy). Leave the flag off. This package becomes the integration spike. The portal script is still worth running (step 4).
- **"US-only, no tax"**. Leave the flag off and run the portal script. Non-US customers can still pay, and that is the compliance risk the decision names.

## 2. Accountant: where you must register (decision, outside Stripe)

Stripe Tax only calculates. It collects tax only where you add a registration, and filing stays with you or your accountant. Bring the accountant your country of incorporation, expected customer countries and volumes, and ask specifically about:

- EU VAT on digital services sold to consumers: for a seller outside the EU, usually from the first sale, through the non-Union OSS scheme.
- UK VAT on digital services: usually from the first sale for a seller outside the UK.
- India GST on online services (OIDAR). The pricing plan targets India-based applicants.
- US sales tax by state. Economic nexus thresholds vary, and Stripe Tax's "Threshold monitoring" shows where you approach one.
- Which product tax code fits Kairos. Stripe's "Software as a service (SaaS) - personal use" (`txcd_10103000`) is the likely one.

Reply with the list of registrations to add, or ask for help reading Stripe's threshold report.

## 3. Activate Stripe Tax in test mode

Stripe dashboard, **test mode** > Settings > Tax (menu labels may differ slightly):

1. Head office address: your business address.
2. Preset product tax code: the code from step 2.
3. Default tax behavior / "Include tax in prices": **No (exclusive)**. Prices stay $19, $39 and $79 plus tax.
4. Registrations: add one EU country (for example Ireland or Germany, or the non-Union OSS registration), so the step 5 test shows tax. Test-mode registrations do not file anything.

## 4. Run the portal script in test mode

From the repo, in Git Bash:

```bash
cd server
read -rs STRIPE_SECRET_KEY && export STRIPE_SECRET_KEY   # paste the sk_test_... key (or a restricted key: Prices read, Customer portal write), then Enter
export STRIPE_PRICES='<the STRIPE_PRICES value from Railway sd1-api, staging>'
npm run stripe:portal              # dry run: prints "(test mode)", any warnings, and each field that would change
npm run stripe:portal -- --apply   # writes them; prints "applied"
npm run stripe:portal              # prints "no changes": the portal is reproducible from the script
```

- "no default customer portal configuration": open Settings > Billing > Customer portal in test mode, click Save once, and rerun.
- A `warning: ... tax_behavior is unspecified, not exclusive` line: fix step 3.3, or set the price's tax behavior to exclusive on the price itself.
- A `warning: ... is in <currency>, not usd` line: that price is wrong for decision 7. Replace it (WP-10 step 5).
- "Limit customers to one subscription" (WP-10 step 9) is not in the API. Keep it on in the dashboard.

## 5. Test-mode acceptance on staging

1. Railway > staging `sd1-api` > Variables > `STRIPE_AUTOMATIC_TAX` = `1`. Redeploy.
2. Sign in on staging, Billing > Starter monthly. On Stripe's Checkout page enter an address in the country you registered in step 3.4, no tax id, and card `4242 4242 4242 4242`.
3. Pass: the Checkout page shows a tax line on top of $19.00 before you pay. The paid invoice in Stripe shows the same tax, `GET /api/me` shows Starter with 1,000 credits, and the webhook deliveries are all 200.
4. Optional: repeat with a US address in a state where you have no registration. Tax is $0.00, which is correct.
5. Upgrade check, matching runbook section 3: open the portal (Billing > Change plan) and switch Starter monthly to Pro monthly. Stripe charges the prorated difference at once, and `GET /api/me` shows Pro with the credit balance unchanged. On a test clock, advancing past the renewal grants 1,200. Switching back to Starter shows as scheduled for the end of the period.

## 6. Dunning settings (test and live)

Stripe > Billing > Revenue recovery (Retries, Emails), as in `docs/runbook/billing.md` section 4. Turn on Smart Retries over about 2 weeks, emails for failed payments and expiring cards, and cancel the subscription after the last retry.

To back out: `STRIPE_AUTOMATIC_TAX=0` and redeploy. New checkouts stop adding tax, and subscriptions already taxed keep it until you change them in Stripe.

## 8. Confirm the upgrade credit rule (decision)

Today, an upgrade on the same interval charges the prorated difference at once and grants no new credits until the next renewal. That renewal grants the new plan's full cycle. A change to a longer interval grants the new price's full cycle at once (runbook section 3). This is what the code did before WP-47 too, and gap-map billing-65 notes it was never confirmed.

Reply **"confirmed"**, or **"grant the difference at upgrade"**. The second needs a code change in `invoiceGrant`: for example, Starter to Pro monthly would grant 200 more credits when the proration invoice is paid.
